GROUND-PENETRATING RESCUE ROBOTS

See beyond
rubble.
Act together.

What if rescue robots could help us understand what lies beneath collapsed structures—and bring that knowledge directly to the people making decisions on the ground?

Follow the mission ↓

The answers may be buried
beneath the rubble.

A collapsed building reveals its destruction from above. But the spaces that matter most for rescue—hidden cavities, possible passages, and unstable structures—remain out of sight.

Send robots
to gather the clues.

Aerial and legged robots bring cameras and ground-penetrating sensors to accessible parts of the collapse, gathering evidence about spaces beyond direct view.

Piece together
what lies inside.

Radar echoes offer clues about the structure beneath the debris. Combined with observations of the surface, those clues help us reason about where voids may exist—and how they might connect.

But a single observation may support
more than one explanation.

Find
the next clue.

When the hidden structure remains uncertain, the next measurement matters. Robots can use what they currently understand to decide where another scan would be most informative.

From hidden spaces
to actionable guidance.

The robot's understanding of hidden spaces becomes spatial guidance that responders can use directly at the scene.

One spatial understanding, two complementary ways of sharing it.

Responders decide.
Robots adapt.

Spatial guidance gives responders a shared understanding of the scene. Their questions, priorities, and constraints then shape what the robots investigate next.
