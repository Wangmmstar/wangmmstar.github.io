Where should rescuers search beneath the rubble?

Robots equipped with ground-penetrating radar can help locate hidden spaces inside collapsed buildings, giving rescue teams information about where to investigate next.

Concept visualization

A collapsed building can hide spaces where people may survive.

Survivors may be trapped in cavities beneath collapsed floors and debris. Responders can see the damage from outside, but cannot easily determine where these hidden spaces are.

Robots can scan for spaces hidden beneath the debris.

Aerial and ground robots offer different ways to reach and examine a collapse site. Equipped with suitable sensors, they can collect ground-penetrating radar measurements from accessible locations.

Platform examples from the supplied proposal; GPR integration and testing in disaster rubble are not established for the pictured robots.

A radar echo is not a rescue map.

Ground-penetrating radar produces indirect measurements, not photographs of the interior. These signals must be interpreted to estimate where voids may exist and how large they could be.

The original studies and acquisition or validation contexts of these figures still require verification. They illustrate signal interpretation, not demonstrated void detection in disaster rubble.

Show responders where hidden spaces may be.

Spatial visualization can place estimated void locations into the context of the collapsed structure. Instead of interpreting radar signals, responders can examine possible cavities and decide where further investigation is needed.

Let rescue priorities guide the next scan.

The longer-term goal is to make robotic sensing responsive to rescue teams. When the available evidence is insufficient, responders could identify what they need to know, and robots could plan additional measurements to reduce that uncertainty.

From hidden spaces to informed rescue decisions.
