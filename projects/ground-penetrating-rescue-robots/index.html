How can robots show rescuers where hidden voids may lie?

Ground-penetrating sensors can help robots estimate hidden spaces beneath rubble. Laser projection could then bring those locations into view for rescuers at the scene.

The spaces rescuers need to find are hidden beneath the rubble.

When a building collapses, cavities may remain between floors and debris. These spaces can provide room for trapped survivors, but rescuers cannot easily see where they are from the surface.

Robots can scan what cameras cannot see.

Cameras show the surface of the rubble. Robots equipped with ground-penetrating radar could collect measurements that provide clues about spaces hidden below it.

Radar signals must be turned into possible void locations.

Radar reflections do not directly show the interior of a collapsed building. By interpreting these measurements, a system can estimate where voids may exist and how they might extend beneath the debris.

A location on a map is not a mark on the rubble.

Even when a void has been located digitally, rescuers still need to identify where it corresponds to the physical scene. A robot-mounted laser projection system could place a visible cue on the rubble surface, giving the team a shared reference for further investigation.

What should the robot investigate next?

Rescuers may need to answer different questions as new evidence emerges. They can identify what matters next, while the robot selects an appropriate follow-up measurement.
