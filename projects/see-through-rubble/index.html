01 / The challenge

What lies beneath cannot be seen from above.

Rubble hides potential survivor spaces. Surface observations alone cannot reveal their location, shape, or depth.

02 / The core idea

From invisible signals
to visible spaces.

We turn GPR measurements into 3D void geometry, then reveal it in the responder’s view through augmented reality.

03 / The AR experience

See the hidden space
in context.

AR overlays reconstructed voids on the rubble, showing their estimated shape, location, depth, and volume.

04 / How it works

Sense. Reconstruct. Reveal.

01

Sense beneath the rubble

Multiple ground-penetrating radar scans capture subsurface reflections across the collapsed structure.

Read more: GPR void detection and localization ↗Hu et al. · Advanced Engineering Informatics, 2019

02

Reconstruct the hidden geometry

Void boundaries extracted from individual radargrams are combined to generate a three-dimensional representation of potential buried spaces.

Read more: 3D void reconstruction ↗Hu et al. · Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering, 2022

03

Reveal the invisible

An image-based augmented reality interface registers reconstructed void information with the disaster scene, making the hidden geometry visible in context.

Read more: Interactive AR visualization ↗Hu et al. · Journal of Computing in Civil Engineering, 2022

06 / Experimental evaluation

Can the hidden spaces be reconstructed and revealed?

The approach was evaluated using simulated collapse scenarios and a scaled physical experiment. Additional tests assessed image-based AR tracking under changes in lighting and occlusion.
