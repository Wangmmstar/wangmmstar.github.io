The bracket is in the model.
The work happens on a wall.

An AC bracket may already have a defined location in BIM. At the workface, someone still has to identify the right target, place its hole pattern on the wall, and document what happened.

The challenge is keeping those steps tied to the same installation target.

Ask for a bracket, not its coordinates.

“Mark the four drill holes for the AC bracket on the west wall.”

The co-pilot matches the worker’s request to an approved BIM-derived target, rather than generating installation coordinates from language alone. If the request is ambiguous, it asks for clarification.

The right layout has to reach the right wall.

Once the target is selected, the mobile platform moves into position at the corresponding workface.

Using the prepared site map and local wall references, the camera–projector unit establishes a new mapping before displaying the installation pattern.

The projected layout becomes a reference for inspection.

After the worker completes the layout, the co-pilot compares the visible markings with the projected hole pattern.

Misplaced or missing features can trigger corrective guidance, while insufficient evidence prompts recapture. The outcome remains linked to the original BIM target.

Toward a robotic site superintendent.

The current co-pilot already connects field guidance, visual verification, and reporting for individual installation tasks.

The next step is site-wide coordination: managing interdependent tasks across workfaces, identifying what needs attention next, and maintaining an up-to-date picture of construction progress.
