How does a robot know what to disinfect in a hospital?

Simulation

A hospital room is not one surface.

A bedrail, a door handle, a mattress, and a countertop are used in different ways. Treating a room as a uniform area ignores which objects people interact with and how a robot can physically reach them.

What is happening—and what is in the room?

Human activities

We trained a visual model to classify 25 types of hospital activity from 5,770 images. It achieved 90.6% classification accuracy in the reported evaluation.

Hospital objects

We created the Hospital Indoor Object Detection (HIOD) dataset with 4,417 images, 51,869 annotated objects, and 56 object categories. Object detection helps identify equipment and furniture in hospital scenes.

A bedrail is not a bedsheet.

A handle is grasped; a tabletop supports objects; bedding is made of fabric. Our affordance and material recognition studies investigate how parts of a scene are used and what those surfaces are made of—information that can inform disinfection planning.

Finding a surface is not the same as reaching it.

Semantic mapping places objects within a spatial representation of the environment. Separate physical robot-arm experiments examine how a treatment tool can approach different surfaces and follow their geometry.
