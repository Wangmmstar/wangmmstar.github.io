How can we map buried pipes without digging?

The problem

GPR records reflections, not a map.

Ground-penetrating radar can scan below the surface, but its measurements do not directly reveal pipe geometry or show where detected pipes lie on a site map. Both interpretation and spatial localization are needed.

From radar reflections to buried pipes.

Instead of detecting pipe-shaped reflections directly, we use deep learning to reconstruct subsurface permittivity maps from GPR scans. GAN-enhanced simulated radargrams provide training data for estimating pipe locations and dimensions.

Orange circles indicate the actual pipe locations used as references, not additional detections.

Locating buried pipes on the site.

Camera-based SLAM tracks the GPR survey path, allowing detected pipe positions to be placed within a reconstructed aboveground environment.

Outdoor field demonstration

An outdoor GPR survey demonstrated how subsurface detections can be combined with camera-based localization to position buried pipes relative to the reconstructed site.

Toward infrastructure-aware construction.

Future research could bring surface and subsurface mapping together to support excavation planning and robotic operations, while addressing uncertainty in underground detection and localization.
